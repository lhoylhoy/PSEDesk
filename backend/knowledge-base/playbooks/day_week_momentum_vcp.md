## Regime first: only hunt in a tradable tape

Momentum works when the index is above its 50-day average and breadth is expanding. The
first question for every DAY and WEEK idea is not "which stock" but "is this a tape that
pays breakout buyers".

- PSEi above a rising 50-day and 200-day average: full risk budget, breakouts hold.
- PSEi between the averages or flat: half risk budget, take only the cleanest pivots and
  reduce the target to the first resistance shelf.
- PSEi below a falling 50-day average: no new swing longs. Counter-trend bounces in a
  downtrend fail at the 20-day average on the first or second attempt.
- Net foreign selling for several consecutive sessions caps rallies in the large caps
  that dominate the index; the opportunity then rotates to second-liners with their own
  catalyst.

A breakout is only as good as the market that receives it. When the index is heavy, a
textbook setup becomes a lower-probability trade with the same stop distance.

## Weinstein stage analysis

Classify the name before you look at entries. Stage analysis is the filter that keeps the
desk out of "cheap" stocks that are structurally broken.

- **Stage 1 — basing.** Price flattens after a decline, the 150-day average flattens and
  volume dries up. Accumulation territory, but there is no edge until a breakout.
- **Stage 2 — advancing.** Price above a rising 150-day average, higher highs and higher
  lows, volume expanding on up weeks. This is the only stage where swing longs have a
  statistical tailwind.
- **Stage 3 — topping.** Price chops across a flattening average, failed breakouts,
  distribution volume. Stop adding; tighten stops on existing positions.
- **Stage 4 — declining.** Price below a falling 150-day average. Every bounce is a
  selling opportunity; long ideas here are knife catches.

A Stage 4 name needs a Stage 1 base — weeks of sideways repair with drying volume —
before it can become a Stage 2 leader. Time, not a single good session, does that work.

## Volatility contraction: the VCP

Mark Minervini's volatility contraction pattern is the desk's preferred swing setup
because it makes the risk measurable. The stock advances, then pulls back in a series of
progressively tighter swings while volume contracts, and finally breaks out through the
pivot on expanding volume.

Characteristics worth screening for:

- Two to four contractions, each shallower than the last (for example 12%, then 7%, then
  4%) with the final pullback holding above the 20-day average.
- Volume drying up into the pivot — the final few sessions should be the quietest of the
  base.
- Relative strength: the stock holds up while the index pulls back, and it is within
  roughly 10% of its 52-week high, not 40% below it.
- A pivot that is a real level (the high of the last contraction), not a round number.

The stop belongs just below the last contraction low. That is what makes the setup
tradable: a tight, structural invalidation point close to entry.

## Pivot breakouts and volume confirmation

A breakout without volume is a drift, and drifts fail. The confirmation rules the desk
applies:

- Breakout volume at least 1.5x the 20-day average, ideally in the first hour rather than
  in a late-session spike.
- Price closes in the upper half of the day's range, above the pivot — not a spike that
  gives it all back.
- The pivot was not already tested and rejected twice; a third attempt at the same level
  is usually where supply wins.
- A gap-up open far above the pivot (more than about one ATR) is a chase, not an entry.
  The stop then sits too far away for the reward that remains.

## Pullbacks into the 20-day average

When the tape is choppy, buying strength is expensive and buying the first orderly
pullback works better than chasing breakouts.

- Entry zone: the 20-day exponential average, or the 50-day simple average in a slower
  trend, with the trend still intact (average rising, higher lows preserved).
- Quality of the pullback: three to six sessions, declining volume, no distribution day
  closing at the lows.
- Invalidation: a close below the swing low that started the leg, not below an arbitrary
  percentage.
- Catalyst alignment: an earnings print already digested, or a sector tailwind — not a
  pullback in a name that just missed estimates.

## When the setup has already failed

Exit discipline matters more than entry selection because the losses are what compound
against you. The setup is dead when:

- Price closes below the contraction low or the pivot that it supposedly broke out from.
- The breakout day's range is fully retraced within two sessions on heavier volume.
- Volume dries up to a fraction of average while price stalls at the pivot — the buyers
  never showed up.
- A new adverse disclosure lands (SRO, profit warning, suspension notice). Technical
  levels do not survive capital-structure events.

The desk prefers a named, structural failure point over a fixed percentage stop, because
a percentage stop and a structure stop can be three ticks apart and one of them is a
donation.
